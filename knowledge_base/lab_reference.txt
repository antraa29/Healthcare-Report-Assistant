HbA1c reflects average blood sugar levels over the past 2-3 months. A normal range is typically below 5.7%. Levels between 5.7% and 6.4% suggest prediabetes, while 6.5% or higher on repeated tests may indicate diabetes. Elevated HbA1c means blood sugar has been running high over time, not just at a single moment.

Fasting Blood Sugar (Glucose) measures blood sugar after at least 8 hours without food. Normal fasting glucose is generally 70-99 mg/dL. Levels of 100-125 mg/dL suggest prediabetes, and 126 mg/dL or above on repeated tests may indicate diabetes. Low readings (hypoglycemia) can cause shakiness, dizziness, or confusion.

Uric Acid is a waste product from the breakdown of purines in food and cells. Normal range is roughly 3.5-7.2 mg/dL for adults. High uric acid (hyperuricemia) is linked to gout and kidney stones. Low levels are less common and usually not clinically significant on their own.

Vitamin D (25-Hydroxyvitamin D) supports bone health, calcium absorption, and immune function. Levels below 20 ng/mL are generally considered deficient, 20-30 ng/mL insufficient, and above 30 ng/mL sufficient. Deficiency is common and linked to fatigue, bone pain, and weakened immunity.

Vitamin B12 is essential for nerve function and red blood cell production. Normal range is roughly 200-900 pg/mL. Low B12 can cause fatigue, weakness, tingling in hands/feet, and memory issues, and is common in vegetarians/vegans and older adults.

Prolactin is a hormone mainly associated with milk production but present in all people. Normal range is roughly 4-23 ng/mL in non-pregnant adults. Elevated prolactin can be caused by stress, certain medications, or pituitary conditions, and may affect menstrual cycles or fertility.

TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) regulates thyroid function. Normal range is roughly 0.4-4.0 mIU/L. High TSH usually indicates an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism), while low TSH usually indicates an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism).
